**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

What must be true before intelligent physical action is permitted?

## Signature mental model

Risk reduction and authorization are independent gates; performance cannot substitute for assurance.

## Thinking practice

Reason from hazards, affected parties and candidate controls to needed evidence; recognize where qualified specialists are required.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Identify hazards in normal operation and non-routine activities, including security-related failure paths.
2. Distinguish candidate risk controls, verification evidence and authorization decisions.
3. Explain why product category, jurisdiction, configuration and lifecycle changes matter for assurance.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Hazard, risk, foreseeable misuse and affected parties
- Risk assessment, protective functions, degraded modes and recovery
- Performance evidence versus safety evidence; independent acceptance gates
- Cyber-physical security, permissions, privacy and controlled updates
- Ethics, accountability, workforce and relevant specialist responsibilities

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Construct a qualitative hazard and responsibility map
- Trace an illustrative sensor or communications fault through a physical workflow
- State missing evidence and distinguish proposed safeguards from validated safeguards

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Introduction to hazard analysis methods and safety cases
- Sector-specific safety standards and conformity pathways; consult current official sources and qualified experts

## Transfer to a new context

Compare a factory robot and a public-space mobile system without assuming identical compliance rules.

## Readiness check

Does not claim safety from task success or waive unresolved required safeguards.
