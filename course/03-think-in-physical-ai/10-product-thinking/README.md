## Central question

How does physical intelligence become a valuable product or service?

## Signature mental model

The complete operated system is the unit of product value; minimize the breadth of the promise, not essential completeness.

## Thinking practice

Translate evidence about work into a bounded product promise, compare minimum concepts and define readiness decisions.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Specify a bounded product promise, supported conditions, exclusions, human/service responsibilities and acceptance criteria.
2. Distinguish a capability demonstration, a minimum concept, a valuable product and a repeatable offering.
3. Apply Done, Dependable, Desired and Duplicated (4D) criteria while treating safety and authorization separately.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Physical outcome products, capability products and enabling infrastructure
- Work discovery, value proposition, bounded promise and operating envelope
- Minimum Product Thinking: Physical MAC/MAP concepts from the companion book
- 4D readiness: Done, Dependable, Desired, Duplicated; evidence maturity versus attainment
- Hardware/software/model lifecycle; NPI, pilots, integration, service and repeatability

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Convert a demonstration into a bounded offering
- Specify evidence required for a next-stage product decision
- Compare product, configurable solution and custom engineering service

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Prototype/EVT/DVT/PVT concepts; design for manufacturing and service
- Forward-deployed product development, data flywheels and partner strategies
