**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

Can intelligent physical systems create sustainable human and economic value?

## Signature mental model

Measure the cost and value of accepted work; capacity released is not automatically cash saved.

## Thinking practice

Model customer and supplier economics separately, compare alternatives and test assumptions under uncertainty.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Calculate effective productive time and cost per accepted outcome with explicit assumptions.
2. Identify capital, installation, service, maintenance, human assistance, energy and rework costs.
3. Compare purchase, rental, service and outcome-based models and explain risk allocation and sensitivities.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Robonomics: accepted work, throughput, quality, labor and capacity
- Availability, productive utilization, coverage and workflow bottlenecks
- Total lifecycle cost, cost-to-serve and customer/provider economics
- Business models: sale, lease, RaaS, per-hour, per-task and licensing
- Break-even, sensitivity, willingness to pay and investment decision criteria

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Build a spreadsheet comparison using a common outcome unit
- Compare headline scheduled rates with productive hours and accepted tasks
- Identify what assumptions would reverse an investment recommendation

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Cash flow, depreciation, capital utilization and contribution margin
- Service capacity, downtime risk and outcome contract design
