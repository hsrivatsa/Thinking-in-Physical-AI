**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

How do intelligent machines improve, scale and participate in larger systems?

## Signature mental model

Earn generality and scale through evidence; collective behavior is not the sum of individual intelligence.

## Thinking practice

Reason across time and scale; plan controlled changes, fleet coordination, replication and human consequences.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Explain the difference between operational adaptation, model learning and authorized releases.
2. Identify integration, service, coordination and correlated failure challenges when scaling to multiple sites or machines.
3. Defend a lifecycle decision to expand, constrain, redesign or discontinue a system, accounting for human and societal effects.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Monitoring, maintenance, calibration, drift, failure harvesting and regression evidence
- Versioned software, models, hardware and configuration; staged updates and change control
- Repeatable deployment, manufacturing, support and site-specific variation
- Multi-robot coordination, shared knowledge, domain-specific brains and correlated failures
- Human-machine workforces, intelligent environments, societal impacts and responsible retirement

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Compare single-machine and fleet operations
- Analyze an illustrative shared-resource or correlated fault scenario
- Prepare a lifecycle and replication decision using evidence gathered across the course

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Collective intelligence, distributed orchestration, multi-agent coordination and emergent behavior
- Human-robot societies, governance, workforce transformation and long-term research questions
