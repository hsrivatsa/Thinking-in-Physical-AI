**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

How do body, intelligence and environment shape one another?

## Signature mental model

Embodiment creates both affordances and constraints; form follows work and environment.

## Thinking practice

Compare complete physical arrangements, including changes to the environment and non-robotic alternatives.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Relate reach, payload, precision, mobility, grip, power and duty cycle to physical tasks.
2. Compare fixed, wheeled, legged and task-specific embodiments using explicit requirements.
3. Justify an embodiment or environmental change with trade-offs rather than novelty.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Embodiment, morphology, degrees of freedom and affordances
- Sensors, actuators, end-effectors, energy, compute and service access
- Navigation, manipulation, reach, balance, payload, accuracy and repeatability
- Task-shaped bodies, mobile robots, arms, humanoids, drones and intelligent infrastructure

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Compare a conveyor, mobile robot, arm, humanoid and manual process
- Use a simple reach and clearance diagram to expose infeasible arrangements
- Evaluate standardized objects, fixtures, lighting and handoff changes

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Kinematics and workspace geometry
- Contact mechanics, grip force, power and thermal constraints

## Transfer to a new context

Compare alternative ways to harvest fruit or assist a person with mobility.

## Readiness check

Can explain why a simpler body or environment change may outperform a more flexible machine.
