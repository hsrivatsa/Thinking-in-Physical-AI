## Central question

What changes when intelligence enters the physical world?

## Signature mental model

Intelligence must answer to physics; intended action is not the same as achieved physical effect.

## Thinking practice

Predict physical consequences from first principles; distinguish a command, an action and a verified state change.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Distinguish Physical AI, digital AI, traditional automation, passive sensing and teleoperation using examples and boundary cases.
2. Explain how forces, contact, energy, time and uncertainty constrain a physical action.
3. Trace a closed feedback loop and distinguish immediate correction from learning across experiences.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Physical AI, embodied AI and cyber-physical systems: definitions and overlaps
- Space, geometry, gravity, friction, inertia, contact and energy: intuitive physical constraints
- Sense → estimate → decide → act → observe/verify: feedback and physical state change
- Partial observations, reversible and irreversible effects, and consequences of error

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Predict outcomes for different objects and surfaces
- Compare a digital instruction with physical execution and verification
- Identify information needed before acting and evidence needed afterward

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Simple state-transition and energy models; quantitative force and friction intuition
- Causality, uncertainty and closed-loop behavior
