**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

How should humans and machines share control, authority and responsibility?

## Signature mental model

Capability does not confer authority; autonomy is an allocation of responsibility.

## Thinking practice

Specify supported conditions, decision rights, interaction, escalation and recovery; challenge overtrust and hidden human burden.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Separate autonomy over job selection, planning, execution, verification, authorization, exceptions and recovery.
2. Specify an operating envelope and responsibility map for a shared physical workflow.
3. Design legible interaction and human intervention with clear triggers and account for assistance burden.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Manual, teleoperated, assisted, supervised and bounded autonomous operation
- Authority release, operating envelope and sector-specific ODD terminology
- Human-robot interaction, legibility, trust calibration, ergonomics and accessibility
- Exceptions, escalation, handoff, intervention, recovery and return-to-service authorization
- Workforce participation, augmentation, substitution and hidden labor

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Allocate responsibilities in a hospital or warehouse delivery scenario
- Write bounded instructions and test how a simulated system handles ambiguity
- Compare assistance demand under two autonomy profiles

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Shared control and supervisory interfaces
- Assistance capacity, remote-support latency and human factors evaluation
