**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

What intelligence is needed, and how should we collaborate with it?

## Signature mental model

Use appropriate intelligence at the appropriate layer; a plausible plan is not physical competence or permission.

## Thinking practice

Compare architectures, reason counterfactually about actions and communicate bounded goals with verifiable outcomes.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Distinguish classical algorithms, learned policies, VLMs, VLAs and world models at a conceptual level.
2. Allocate reasoning, prediction, action and protection to appropriate system components.
3. Specify a human instruction, context, action constraints and verification criteria; challenge the resulting plan.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Reactive, deliberative, predictive, classical, learned and hybrid intelligence
- Perception-to-action representations; skill policies and hierarchical planning
- Language and vision models, vision-language-action models and world models
- Counterfactual prediction, uncertainty, preconditions and physical feasibility
- Human goal communication, agentic orchestration, skill contracts and independent protective functions

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Compare rule-based, learned and hybrid approaches for replenishment
- Give a simulated agent a goal with and without context; critique its plan
- Compare alternative predicted outcomes and define acceptance checks

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Action representations and policy learning; cross-embodiment adaptation
- Domain-specific robotic brains and orchestration; model limits and validation

Choose intelligence approaches for an adaptive building or agricultural inspection system.
