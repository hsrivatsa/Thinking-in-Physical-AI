# Module 06. Experiential Thinking

**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

How can machines acquire and improve physical skills through experience?

## Signature mental model

Experience shapes capability; diverse, representative evaluation establishes its limits.

## Thinking practice

Design a learning/data experiment and distinguish training experience, transfer evidence and controlled improvement.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Explain imitation learning, reinforcement learning and demonstrations at a conceptual level.
2. Identify data coverage gaps, embodiment differences, privacy and collection costs.
3. Design a staged simulation-to-reality evaluation and explain why simulation is not deployment proof.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Learning from demonstrations, imitation, rewards, exploration and feedback
- Do as I do: demonstration, teleoperation, video and embodiment transfer
- Trajectories, sensor streams, labels, failures, consent and physical data logistics
- Simulation, digital twins, domain randomization and sim-to-real discrepancy
- Fleet feedback, data curation and controlled policy/model releases

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Compare three ways to acquire a grasp-and-place skill
- Identify missing operating conditions in a demonstration dataset
- Evaluate a trained behavior against held-out scenarios and revise the data plan

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Behavioral cloning and reinforcement learning notebooks
- System identification, domain adaptation and cross-embodiment data

## Transfer to a new context

Design a learning strategy for a robot handling irregular produce or an adaptive assistive device.

## Readiness check

Can explain why more training data alone is not proof of generalization or safety.
