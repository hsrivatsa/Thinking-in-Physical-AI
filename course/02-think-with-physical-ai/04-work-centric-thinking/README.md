**Status:** outline available; lesson, lab, assignment and assessment in development.

## Central question

What meaningful physical work should intelligent systems perform?

## Signature mental model

Start with the work and accepted outcome, not the robot or model.

## Thinking practice

Observe the complete work cycle, establish a baseline, decompose work and compare alternatives.

## Observable learning outcomes

1. Map a physical workflow from demand to accepted completion, including exceptions and hidden work.
2. Define a measurable operational problem and affected stakeholders.
3. Select a bounded opportunity and explain when a non-AI alternative is better.

## Curriculum outline

### Core — required concepts

- Purpose, jobs, workflows, tasks, skills, actions and physical effects
- Field discovery: buyers, operators, maintainers, nearby workers and supervisors
- Current process, baseline, variations, bottlenecks, workarounds and exceptions
- Task eligibility, acceptance criteria, alternative solutions and initial operating scope

### Applied — investigation and judgment

- Map one ordinary work process with observations or a provided case
- Compare human assistance, conventional automation, workflow redesign and Physical AI
- Identify the highest-impact uncertain assumption to test

### Extension — optional technical or advanced depth

- Task/environment complexity and consequence-of-failure screening; separate risk gate
- Deployment similarity and opportunity segmentation

## Transfer to a new context

Apply the method to hospital supply restocking or food preparation.

## Readiness check

Can identify meaningful work without assuming a robot is the answer.
