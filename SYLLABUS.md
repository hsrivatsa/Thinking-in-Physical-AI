# Course syllabus — proposed design

[Course home](README.md) · [Detailed course outline](COURSE-OUTLINE.md)

## Course description

Artificial intelligence is moving from digital information processing into systems that sense, interpret and act in the physical world. These systems must contend with imperfect observations, gravity, contact, time, limited energy, human interaction and potentially irreversible consequences.

**Thinking in Physical AI** develops the mental models, investigative practices and judgment needed to understand, work with and responsibly design intelligent physical systems. It is not a robot-programming bootcamp or a condensed product-management textbook. It integrates AI, robotics, systems thinking, human factors, experimentation, product value, economics and governance through a progression from understanding to application to independent reasoning.

The central idea: a machine's capability is not proof of dependable work, permission to act or sustainable value. Learn to reason about the complete operated system—machine, intelligence, environment, people, workflow, service and economics—and to decide what should be done, under which conditions, with what evidence and with whose responsibility.

## Abstract

Physical AI brings intelligence into physically situated, feedback-driven systems. Unlike purely digital interaction, physical action changes the world and can have difficult-to-reverse effects. Embodiment, observability, uncertainty, physics, time, human responsibilities and deployment economics therefore shape what an intelligent system can accomplish and what it should be permitted to do. The course introduces an integrated way of reasoning organized around twelve thinking disciplines and three learning stages: Think ABOUT, Think WITH and Think IN Physical AI. Through experiments, comparative cases, critical reflection and a cumulative capstone, learners will connect physical intuition to technical understanding, evidence-based decision-making, assurance and human purpose. The intended outcome is independent applied judgment, not professional robotics engineering certification.

## Audience and prerequisites

Primary: product builders, AI/software professionals, robotics founders, engineers and designers. Additional audiences: students, operations professionals, business, workforce and policy leaders. The core assumes basic computer literacy and curiosity; technical extensions are optional. No robot or paid tool required by design.

## Format and planned workload

Twelve sequential modules, adaptable to self-paced learning or a twelve-week facilitated cohort. A proposed weekly allocation is 90 minutes of concept study, 90 minutes of studio work and 1–3 hours of independent practice including the cumulative project (48–72 hours total). These are planning assumptions; lab duration, workload and mentoring capacity must be tested. Optional extensions are additional. No instructor or mentor service is currently provided by this public shell.

## Observable learning outcomes

| Outcome | Evidence of learning | Primary modules |
|---|---|---|
| Explain the physical intelligence loop and constraints | Physical loop map and prediction/observation record | 01–03 |
| Compare embodiment and environmental alternatives | Feasibility and architecture analysis | 02–03 |
| Diagnose uncertainty, perception, feedback and interface failures | State/observation comparison and failure-path analysis | 01, 03, 05 |
| Frame meaningful physical work and compare non-AI alternatives | Baseline, workflow map and bounded opportunity | 04 |
| Select intelligence and experience strategies | Architecture and held-out learning experiment plan | 05–06 |
| Design human collaboration and delegated authority | Authority and exception map | 07–08 |
| Identify hazards and distinguish assurance from performance | Hazard/evidence register and decision to defer where needed | 08–09 |
| Evaluate claims with explicit conditions, denominators and limits | Evaluation plan and comparative evidence record | 09 |
| Define product scope and readiness | Bounded promise and 4D assessment | 10 |
| Analyze cost and value of accepted work | Customer/provider model and sensitivity analysis | 11 |
| Reason about updates, coordination, replication and human impacts | Lifecycle and fleet decision | 12 |
| Transfer reasoning to an unfamiliar situation and defend a decision | Portfolio, practical capstone and oral or written defense | All |

## Curriculum and learning sequence

## Teaching method and personal project

Use the [Physical AI Thinking Studio](framework/thinking-studio.md). Start with independent predictions, investigate a model or scenario, challenge it, revise the explanation, make a decision and transfer the practice. Select a personal project by Module 02; complete the [portfolio](portfolio/README.md) and [capstone](capstone/README.md). In a facilitated cohort, a short mentor/peer check-in after each module is proposed; self-study can use written self-review instead.

## Assessment (proposed)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Thinking studios and assignments | 30% |
| Reflection and participation or self-review | 15% |
| Connected portfolio | 20% |
| Capstone report, evidence and demonstration | 25% |
| Oral or written defense | 10% |

Assessment evaluates reasoning, not impressive robot motion. No grade or credential is currently issued. See the [proficiency rubric](framework/assessment-rubric.md). Mandatory evidence integrity and safety/authorization reasoning are independent of numeric marks. A justified decision not to deploy is a successful learning outcome.

## Responsible use of AI and physical systems

Form initial hypotheses independently where practical. Use AI to investigate, tutor, critique or help implement a simulation, then verify claims, reproduce material calculations, disclose substantial AI assistance and explain the reasoning yourself. Never represent generated or hypothetical data as measured evidence. Do not connect a general-purpose AI assistant to physical machinery for uncontrolled experiments. Use safe simulations or supplied traces; hardware work, if later offered, requires suitable facilities, qualified supervision, risk assessment and authorization.

Respect privacy, intellectual property, workplace permissions and the rights of affected people. This course does not authorize physical deployment or establish professional qualifications.
