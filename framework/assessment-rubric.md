# Proposed course proficiency rubric

[Framework](README.md) · [Syllabus](../SYLLABUS.md)

This is an initial curriculum-level rubric, to be calibrated with sample work and pilot learners. Module-specific scored rubrics and answer keys remain planned.

| Competency | Beginner | Developing | Proficient | Advanced extension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical and embodied reasoning | Names components or constraints | Connects constraints to familiar tasks | Predicts and explains effects; compares feasible bodies | Quantifies limits and investigates unexpected behavior |
| Knowledge and systems reasoning | Describes the machine loop | Notices missing observations or dependencies | Distinguishes world, belief and action; diagnoses interfaces | Compares competing causal models with evidence |
| Work and collaboration | Names an automation opportunity | Maps work and human roles | Compares alternatives and defines accepted work and responsibilities | Redesigns workflows and tests human burden |
| Intelligence and learning | Names model families | Describes use and data needs | Justifies architecture and investigates held-out variation | Evaluates transfer and data/compute trade-offs |
| Authority and assurance | Recognizes potential harm | Identifies hazards and permission questions | Bounds authority and separates risk-reduction evidence from performance | Traces lifecycle/configuration changes and specialist evidence needs |
| Evidence reasoning | Reads a success metric | Questions missing conditions and denominators | Designs a representative test and qualifies results | Analyzes confounding, uncertainty and evidence provenance |
| Product and economics | Names features and costs | Includes deployment and support | Compares accepted work, full costs and readiness | Tests sensitivities, risk allocation and replication assumptions |
| Evolution and transfer | Describes a familiar application | Recognizes scaling issues | Applies reasoning to a new domain and defends lifecycle choice | Investigates collective effects and revised strategic decisions |

## Mandatory completion gates

- Evidence is honestly identified as measured, simulated, hypothetical, vendor-reported or inferred.
- No deployment recommendation ignores applicable unresolved safety or authority requirements.
- Credible alternatives and important uncertainties are explicit.
- The learner can independently explain their own reasoning and substantial AI assistance.

These are proposed course gates, not certification criteria. A recommendation to stop, defer or redesign can meet proficiency. Compare an initial diagnostic with a comparable end-of-course transfer task; evaluate change in reasoning, not memorized terminology.
