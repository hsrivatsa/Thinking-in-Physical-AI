# Capstone — design, investigate and defend a Physical AI concept

[Course home](../README.md) · [Portfolio](../portfolio/README.md) · [Project template](project-template.md) · [Evidence record](evidence-template.md)

**Status: proposed capstone specification; detailed examples and scoring guides are planned.**

Choose a real or supplied physical-work problem, a robotic capability or an enabling technology. A non-AI solution is valid if the evidence supports it. Start by Module 02 and use the same project across the three stages.

| Milestone | Modules | Expected work |
|---|---|---|
| Select and understand | 01–03 | Initial diagnostic, problem, body/environment and system model |
| Frame and design | 04–05 | Baseline, alternatives, intelligence and bounded contribution |
| Experiment and challenge | 06–07 | Reproducible safe investigation and human/authority map |
| Establish evidence and limits | 08–09 | Assurance questions, representative/held-out tests, evidence register |
| Establish value and decision | 10–11 | Product readiness, full economics, competing options |
| Integrate and defend | 12 | Lifecycle recommendation, transfer and reflection |

## Required final outputs

A concise capstone report, one-page system and value map, connected portfolio, at least one reproducible practical experiment with limitations, readiness/economic assessment and a presentation with oral or written defense. Show how reasoning changed after evidence; a hypothetical proposal without practical investigation is insufficient. An experiment may be a safe simulation, spreadsheet model, prepared trace analysis or mock digital tool; it must not misrepresent physical validation.

## Assessment

Apply the [course rubric](../framework/assessment-rubric.md). Proposed capstone dimensions: problem 15%, physical/technical reasoning 15%, systems 15%, human collaboration/product design 10%, experimental evidence 20%, economic/operational reasoning 15%, strategic judgment/communication 10%. Evidence integrity and safety/authorization reasoning are independent gates. A decision to stop or narrow scope can be an excellent capstone.

No hardware required. No actual deployment authority, professional qualification or academic credit is granted.
